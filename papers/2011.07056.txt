                                                 Pattern Problems related to the Arithmetic Kakeya Conjecture
                                                 Charlie Cowen-Breen, Elene Karangozishvili, Narmada Varadarajan and Thomas Wang


                                                                                                 Abstract




arXiv:2011.07056v1 [math.NT] 13 Nov 2020
                                                     We study a variety of problems about homothets of sets related to the Kakeya conjecture. In particular,
                                                 we show many of these problems are equivalent to the arithmetic Kakeya conjecture of Katz and Tao.
                                                 We also provide a proof that the arithmetic Kakeya conjecture implies the Kakeya conjecture for packing
                                                 dimension, as this implication was previously only known for Minkowski dimension.
                                                     We consider several questions analogous to the classical results of Stein and Bourgain about the
                                                 Lebesgue measure of a set containing a sphere centered at every point of [0, 1] 𝑛 , where we replace
                                                 spheres by arbitrary polytopes. We give a lower bound for polytopes in R𝑛 , and show that this is sharp
                                                 for simplices. Finally, we generalize number theoretic methods of Green and Ruzsa to study patterns in
                                                 number fields and thereby provide upper bounds for several of these homothet problems.

                                           1. Introduction


                                           1.1   Geometric motivation
                                                Our journey begins with a classical and deep result—discovered first by Stein for 𝑛 ≥ 3, and then
                                           independently by Bourgain [2] and Marstrand [12] for 𝑛 = 2—that any set 𝐵 ⊂ R𝑛 containing a sphere
                                           𝑆 𝑛−1 centered at each point of R𝑛 must have positive Lebesgue measure. This was sharpened by Wolff [20],
                                           [21] and Mitsis [13] to show the following. Suppose that 𝐵 contains a sphere centered at each point of a set
                                           𝑆 ⊂ R𝑛 . If dim 𝐻 𝑆 ≤ 1, then dim 𝐻 𝐵 ≥ 𝑛 − 1 + dim 𝐻 𝑆. If dim 𝐻 𝑆 > 1, then 𝐵 must have positive Lebesgue
                                           measure, hence dim 𝐻 𝐵 = 𝑛. Moreover, Talagrand [17] and Mitsis [13] showed that this is sharp for the
                                           threshold dim 𝐻 𝑆 = 1: there exists a set of Lebesgue measure zero containing a sphere centered at each point
                                           of a straight line 𝑆 in R𝑛 .
                                                Keleti, Nagy, and Shmerkin showed, perhaps surprisingly, that when 𝑛 = 2, these statements do not hold
                                           if the circles are replaced by the boundaries of axis-parallel squares. Namely, for every 𝑠 ∈ [0, 2], there exist
                                           compact sets 𝑆, 𝐵 ⊂ R2 such that 𝐵 contains the boundary of a square centered at each point of 𝑆, dim 𝐻 𝑆 = 𝑠,
                                           and dim 𝐻 𝐵 = 1 ([10]). Moreover, this result holds identically for all polygons and, indeed, generalizes for
                                           all polytopes in R𝑛 due to Chang, Csörnyei, Hera, and Keleti [4].
                                                What does not hold identically for all polygons and polytopes is the corresponding question for Minkowski
                                           and packing dimensions. We will illustrate this by contrasting the results for cubes and simplices. In [19],
                                           Thornton generalized a result of Keleti et al. to show that if 𝐵 ⊂ R𝑛 contains the boundary of an axis-parallel
                                                                                                                                    1
                                           𝑛-cube centered at each point in [0, 1] 𝑛 , then for dim = dim 𝑀 or dim 𝑃 , dim𝐵 ≥ 𝑛 − 2𝑛  , and this is sharp.

Based on these results, one might expect that for a sequence (𝔭 𝑘 ) 𝑘 ∈N of polytopes which converge to the
sphere 𝑆 𝑛−1 (in the Hausdorff metric), the minimal Minkowski or packing dimension of a set 𝐵 ⊂ R𝑛

of all of R𝑛 , as Talagrand and Mitsis did, then the corresponding statement is equivalent to the arithmetic
Kakeya conjecture.

1.2   The Kakeya conjecture
    Each of the problems in the previous section could rightly be classified as a variant of the celebrated
Kakeya conjecture. Here we state two versions of the Kakeya conjecture; a more complete introduction to
the Kakeya conjecture can be found in [18].

Conjecture 1.2 (Kakeya conjecture). A Besicovitch set is a subset of R𝑛 containing a unit line segment in
every direction. If 𝐵 ⊂ R𝑛 is a Besicovitch set, then

  (a) dim 𝐻 𝐵 = 𝑛.

  (b) dim 𝑃 𝐵 = 𝑛.

  (c) dim 𝑀 𝐵 = 𝑛.

1.3     Pattern problems
   The main objective of this paper is to establish a connection between AKC and the sort of problems
considered in section 1.1. Broadly defined, our setup is as follows. Let

                                                 P = {𝔭 𝑥,𝑟 : 𝑥 ∈ 𝑋, 𝑟 ∈ 𝑅}

be a collection of subsets 𝔭 𝑥,𝑟 ⊂ 𝑀 of a (commutative) ring 𝑀, where 𝑋 and 𝑅 are indexing sets. 𝑀 will
typically also be a module over a suitable commutative ring 𝑅. (The canonical example is the structure of R𝑛
as an R-module.)

Problem 1.5 (Pattern problem). Let P be a family as above.

  (a) If 𝐵 contains a set 𝔭 𝑥,𝑟 for every 𝑥 from some subset 𝑆 ⊂ 𝑋, what can we say about the size of 𝐵?

  (b) If 𝐵 contains a set 𝔭 𝑥,𝑟 for every 𝑟 from some subset 𝑆 ⊂ 𝑅, what can we say about the size of 𝐵?

    The notion of size may refer to cardinality, measure, or dimension – whichever yields the most nontrivial
information about 𝐵. Of course, this problem is only interesting when the sets 𝔭 𝑥,𝑟 are themselves of interest.

Definition 1.6. Where addition and multiplication are suitably defined, we say the set 𝑥 + 𝑟 · 𝔭 is a 𝔭-pattern
with basepoint 𝑥 and scaling factor 𝑟.

and scaling factor 𝑑 ∈ Z.1 Similarly, if 𝔭 is a convex polytope in R𝑛 , a homothet 𝑥 + 𝑟 · 𝔭 with 𝑥 ∈ R𝑛 and

    The Kakeya conjecture is a problem of type (b): if 𝐵 ⊂ R𝑛 contains a [0, 1]-pattern (a unit line segment)
